This is one of the hardest operational problems for African e-commerce. The biggest mistake many startups make is treating Cash on Delivery (COD) like online payment. It isn't. **COD is a logistics and financial reconciliation problem**, not a payment problem.

If I were designing your platform (which is especially relevant given your WhatsApp-first marketplace), I would build the entire system around **escrow-like accounting**, even though the money never touches your bank account during the transaction.

---

# Think of COD as a virtual wallet

Instead of saying

> Customer paid cash.

Think

> Delivery agent collected money on behalf of the platform.

The system should behave exactly like online payments except that the **delivery agent becomes the payment gateway.**

The flow becomes

```
Customer
      │
Places Order
      │
Platform
      │
Assign Delivery Agent
      │
Agent collects cash
      │
Money belongs to platform (virtually)
      │
Platform distributes earnings
      │
Vendor
Platform
Agency
Agent commission
```

The accounting is identical to Stripe.

Only the collection method changes.

# Require security deposits or rolling reserves

For new delivery partners, require either:
- A refundable security deposit before they can handle COD.
- Or retain a small percentage (e.g., 5–10%) of their earned commissions as a rolling reserve that is released after a defined period if there are no unresolved discrepancies.

This creates financial accountability without blocking trustworthy partners over the long term.

# Architecture

I would model the financial side with dedicated entities rather than embedding payment state inside the order:

```

Orders

↓

Payment
payment_method
status

↓

Ledger Entries
credit
debit

↓

Settlement

↓

Wallet

↓

Agent Liability

↓

Vendor Payout

```

Every financial movement becomes a ledger entry. Wallet balances are computed from the ledger, not manually edited.

## For your marketplace

Given the marketplace you've described previously—with vendors, delivery agencies, delivery agents, WhatsApp ordering, and support for offline payments—I would avoid letting vendors collect COD directly. The strongest operational model is:

1. The customer pays the delivery agent.
2. The delivery agent is accountable to the platform (or their delivery agency) for the cash collected.
3. The platform records virtual allocation of funds to the vendor, platform fees, agency, and agent immediately after verified delivery.
4. Vendors are paid only after the collected cash has been reconciled and settled.

This approach gives you nearly the same financial visibility and control as online payments while accommodating the realities of cash-based commerce in many African markets. It also scales well because every FCFA is traceable through a verifiable chain of events, liabilities, and settlements rather than relying on trust alone.
